# Related Work (draft for report §2)

> Draft. Citation numbers refer to `references/README.md` and will be renumbered in the final report. Only `[V]`/`[V-abs]` entries are cited for method claims.

Fuzzy C-Means (FCM) [2] assigns every pixel a degree of membership in each cluster and is a standard tool for soft image segmentation, but because it treats pixels independently it is sensitive to noise [1]. The large body of work that followed can be grouped by the kind of spatial information it injects into the clustering process.

**Local spatial information.** BCFCM [8] first added a neighborhood term to the FCM objective for bias-field-corrupted MR images, at a high computational cost. FCM_S1/S2 [9] replaced the neighborhood term with a pre-computed mean- or median-filtered image and a kernel-induced distance, and FGFCM [10] combined spatial and gray-level similarity and clustered on the gray-level histogram to reduce run time. FLICM [11] introduced a parameter-free fuzzy factor that weights neighbors by their spatial distance and gave rise to a family of variants, including the kernel-metric KWFLICM [13] and the adaptive ADFLICM [14]. FRFCM [15] pre-processes the image by morphological reconstruction [6] and filters the membership matrix, while DSFCM_N [16] models the noise as a sparse deviation from the true pixel value. FCM_SICM [18] extracts spatial and intensity information with bilateral filtering and uses membership linking to reduce iterations. A common limitation pointed out in [1] is that these local operators essentially *average* the neighborhood, so when a large fraction of neighbors is itself corrupted the corrected value is biased.

**Non-local and region-level information.** Following non-local means [5], FCM_SNLS [12] collects information from pixels whose surrounding patches are similar, even if they are far away. FALRCM [20] combines local and region-level information with median memberships, and FSC_LNML [22] combines fuzzy subspace clustering with adaptive local variance, non-local information and mean membership linking. Jiao et al. [24] argue that local information alone cannot restore the image at high noise density while non-local information tends to preserve noise and create false edges; they build a weighted image from regional and local filtering and adapt, per pixel, the balance between the original and the weighted image from their difference.

**Transform-domain and sparsity-based methods.** WFCM [17] performs fuzzy clustering in a wavelet-frame domain, and SRFCM [19] adds morphological reconstruction and a sparse regularization of the membership matrix, solved by a Lagrange step followed by thresholding and normalization. Sparsity has also been used in a different sense: Chen et al. [25] embed the sparse self-representation of subspace clustering into fuzzy clustering to capture global correlations in infrared images.

**Other directions.** Recent work modifies the fuzzy partition itself, e.g., harmonic fuzzy partitions with a convergence proof [27]; couples fuzzy clustering with deep autoencoders and kernel distances [26]; or estimates the number of clusters automatically from histogram peaks [28], addressing a limitation that [1] acknowledges.

**Position of the reference paper.** Wu et al. [1] combine the strands above: (i) a local operator that is *not* an average, since neighbors are split by gray-value rank into a reward set (the middle, golden-ratio share) and a penalty set (the extremes) with different weights; (ii) non-local means applied to the dual-tree complex wavelet sub-bands [4] rather than in the spatial domain, to preserve edges; (iii) per-pixel adaptive coefficients for the original, local and non-local terms; and (iv) an ℓ0 sparse regularization of the membership matrix, solved with the three-step scheme of SRFCM [19]. Among the methods summarized in `references/README.md` (Table E), it is the only one that uses all five ingredients (local, non-local, wavelet domain, sparsity, per-pixel adaptive weights); its closest relatives are SRFCM [19] (wavelet + sparsity) and FSC_LNML [22] / Jiao et al. [24] (local + non-local + adaptive weights). The price, according to [1], is a higher computational cost than most FCM variants (O(n⁵) in its own complexity analysis), although it is reported to be faster than FSC_LNML and CGFFCM.
